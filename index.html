Why we began. On our own yatras to Kailash we saw promises made and not kept, and yatris left to manage on their own at the holiest place they would ever visit. We decided to step up and make sure the journey itself becomes a spiritual experience, one a yatri carries for the rest of their life. That is still the whole purpose.

Our volunteers serve from India, Nepal, Malaysia, the United Kingdom, the United States and Australia. The foundation is incorporated in India.

Seva is the first duty of a devotee

A Shiva devotee's foremost role is selfless service. Not service for merit, not service for a name, but service because the one being served is Shiva himself.

The saints of Tamil Shaivism said this eighteen hundred years ago, and the sutras of Kashmir Shaivism say it in another tongue. We keep these verses close. They are the reason the foundation exists.

From Tamil Shaiva literature

அன்பும் சிவமும் இரண்டென்பர் அறிவிலார் அன்பே சிவமாவது ஆரும் அறிகிலார் அன்பே சிவமாவது ஆரும் அறிந்தபின் அன்பே சிவமாய் அமர்ந்திருந்தாரே

Anbum Sivamum irandenbar arivilaar Anbe Sivamaavadhu aarum arigilaar Anbe Sivamaavadhu aarum arindhapin Anbe Sivamaai amarndhirundhaare

Thirumular, Thirumandiram (Tenth Thirumurai), verse 270

The unknowing say that love and Shiva are two things. None of them know that love itself is Shiva. Once a person knows that love itself is Shiva, they remain seated as love, and so as Shiva.

Anbe Sivam. Every act of seva we do is an attempt to live this one line. When love is Shiva, feeding a stranger is worship.

யாவர்க்குமாம் இறைவற்கு ஒரு பச்சிலை யாவர்க்குமாம் பசுவுக்கு ஒரு வாயுறை யாவர்க்குமாம் உண்ணும்போது ஒரு கைப்பிடி யாவர்க்குமாம் பிறர்க்கு இன்னுரை தானே

Yaavarkkumaam iraivarku oru pachchilai Yaavarkkumaam pasuvukku oru vaayurai Yaavarkkumaam unnumpodhu oru kaippidi Yaavarkkumaam pirarkku innurai thaane

Thirumular, Thirumandiram, verse 252

Anyone can offer a single green leaf to the Lord. Anyone can offer a mouthful of grass to a cow. Anyone, when they sit to eat, can set aside a handful for another. Anyone can offer a kind word to a fellow being.

This is the verse behind our annadanam. Thirumular does not ask for grand offerings. He asks for a handful of rice at every meal. Multiply that handful across thousands of devotees and you have a kitchen at the foot of Kailash.

படமாடக் கோயில் பகவற்கு ஒன்று ஈயின் நடமாடக் கோயில் நம்பர்க்கு அங்கு ஆகா நடமாடக் கோயில் நம்பர்க்கு ஒன்று ஈயின் படமாடக் கோயில் பகவற்கு அது ஆமே

Padamaadak kovil Bhagavarku ondru eeyin Nadamaadak kovil Nambarku angu aagaa Nadamaadak kovil Nambarku ondru eeyin Padamaadak kovil Bhagavarku adhu aame

Thirumular, Thirumandiram, verse 1857

What is given to the Lord in the temple of stone does not reach the Lord who lives in the walking temple. But what is given to the Lord in the walking temple, the living human being, reaches the Lord in the temple of stone as well.

The devotee walking the kora with bleeding feet, the child on the roadside in Nepal, the pilgrim at Pashupatinath: each is the walking temple. Serving them is serving Shiva in both temples at once.

நமச்சிவாய வாஅழ்க நாதன் தாள் வாழ்க இமைப்பொழுதும் என் நெஞ்சில் நீங்காதான் தாள் வாழ்க

Namachivaaya vaazhga Naadhan thaal vaazhga Imaippozhudhum en nenjil neengaadhaan thaal vaazhga

Manikkavasagar, Thiruvasagam, Sivapuranam, opening lines (Eighth Thirumurai)

Hail the five letters, Shivaya Nama. Hail the feet of the Lord. Hail the feet of the One who does not leave my heart even for the blink of an eye.

These are the lines our yatris chant at Yam Dwar before the kora begins.

From the Taittiriya Upanishad

The Bhrigu Valli of the Taittiriya Upanishad is the oldest teaching we know on annadanam. The sage Bhrigu is sent by his father Varuna to know Brahman, and the first thing he finds is food.

अन्नं ब्रह्मेति व्यजानात्। अन्नाद्ध्येव खल्विमानि भूतानि जायन्ते। अन्नेन जातानि जीवन्ति। अन्नं प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्तीति।

Annaṁ brahmeti vyajānāt. Annād dhy eva khalv imāni bhūtāni jāyante. Annena jātāni jīvanti. Annaṁ prayanty abhisaṁviśantīti.

Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhrigu Valli 3.2

He knew that food is Brahman. From food alone all beings are born; by food, once born, they live; and into food they return at the end.

When we put a plate in front of a devotee, we are not handing over a thing. We are handing over Brahman.

अन्नं न निन्द्यात्। तद्व्रतम्।

Annaṁ na nindyāt. Tad vratam.

Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhrigu Valli 3.7

Do not speak ill of food. That is the vow.

अन्नं बहु कुर्वीत। तद्व्रतम्।

Annaṁ bahu kurvīta. Tad vratam.

Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhrigu Valli 3.9

Make food plentiful. That is the vow.

The Upanishad does not say feed yourself well. It says multiply food, so that there is enough for everyone who comes.

न कञ्चन वसतौ प्रत्याचक्षीत। तद्व्रतम्। तस्माद्यया कया च विधया बह्वन्नं प्राप्नुयात्। अराध्यस्मा अन्नमित्याचक्षते।

Na kañcana vasatau pratyācakṣīta. Tad vratam. Tasmād yayā kayā ca vidhayā bahv annaṁ prāpnuyāt. Arādhy asmā annam ity ācakṣate.

Taittiriya Upanishad, Bhrigu Valli 3.10

Turn no one away from your dwelling. That is the vow. Therefore, by whatever means, gather food in plenty, and let it be said of you: food is ready here.

This is the vow we have taken at Yam Dwar, at Pashupatinath, at Kasi and at Thiruvannamalai. No devotee turned away. Food is ready here.

From the Shiva Sutras of Vasugupta

The Shiva Sutras were revealed to the sage Vasugupta in Kashmir in the ninth century. They are short, and each one opens like a door.

चैतन्यमात्मा

Caitanyam ātmā

Shiva Sutra 1.1

Consciousness is the Self.

The same awareness looks out of every pair of eyes we serve. There is no one who is not Shiva.

विस्मयो योगभूमिकाः

Vismayo yoga-bhūmikāḥ

Shiva Sutra 1.12

Wonder is the stage of yoga.

Anyone who has seen the North Face of Kailash at dawn knows what this sutra means. We organise yatras so that the wonder is not lost to poor planning.

लोकानन्दः समाधिसुखम्

Lokānandaḥ samādhi-sukham

Shiva Sutra 1.18

The bliss of the world is the joy of samadhi.

The joy of a devotee eating a hot meal at Yam Dwar is not separate from the joy of the yogi. Seva makes the world itself the seat of samadhi.

शरीरवृत्तिर्व्रतम्

Śarīra-vṛttir vratam

Shiva Sutra 3.26

The activity of the body is the vow.

For the one who knows, there is no separate ritual. Cooking, carrying, walking, handing out a raincoat: the body's ordinary work becomes the observance.

दानमात्मज्ञानम्

Dānam ātma-jñānam

Shiva Sutra 3.28

Giving is knowledge of the Self.

The last word on why we serve. To give is not a step toward realisation; giving is realisation.

Kailash

We were the first to take annadanam to Kailash itself. At Yam Dwar, the gateway where the kora begins, we serve food, chocolates and juice to yatris setting out on the parikrama, and to the Tibetan devotees who circle the mountain in full prostration.

For those performing sashtanga danda pradakshana we also carry up what their bodies need to survive the mountain: gloves, knee caps, protective gowns, shoes, beanie caps and raincoats, for adults and for children.

What is sashtanga danda pradakshana?

Sashtanga means eight limbs. Danda here is the danda namaskaram: lying down completely on the earth, the whole body surrendered, the fullest form of namaskaram there is. Pradakshana is the sacred circling of a holy place, keeping it on one's right. Together, the words describe the most demanding form of pilgrimage there is: circling the mountain not on foot, but with the whole body given to the ground the entire way.

The devotee stands, brings the palms together, then lies down in full namaskaram so that eight parts of the body touch the ground: the forehead, the chest, both palms, both knees and both feet. They stretch the arms forward and mark the ground where the fingertips reach. They rise, walk only to that mark, and lie down again. The 52-kilometre kora around Kailash is measured out one body-length at a time.

Walking, the kora takes a fit yatri three days. In prostration it takes three to four weeks, over the Drolma La pass at 5,600 metres, across scree, ice and streams, through snow and hail. Tibetan devotees, including families with children, perform the full parikrama this way. Their gloves wear through. Their knees split. Their aprons shred on the rock.

That is why we carry up gloves, knee caps, gowns, shoes, beanies and raincoats along with the food. We cannot walk their kora for them. We can make sure the mountain does not take their hands and knees before it gives them Shiva.

Kailash Manasarovar Yatra

We organise the yatra so that the yatri can forget the organising. Our aim is one thing: that every yatri has the deepest experience of Kailash it is possible to have.

What makes our yatras different

Most groups complete the outer kora and return. We have, year after year, taken yatris beyond it:

Charan Sparsh at the North Face. Climbing from Dirapuk to the foot of the North Face to touch the mountain itself.

Nandi Parikrama, the inner kora. The circling of Nandi hill inside the outer kora, past the Saptarishi caves and Atmalingam, undertaken by very few.

Every yatra is led by devotees who have done these walks themselves, with a group medical custodian, and with annadanam at Yam Dwar built into the journey.

Who the yatra is for

We are not a travel agency. Kailash Sacred Walks Foundation is a non-profit, and the yatra is organised as seva. There is no margin: the total expense of the yatra is divided equally by the number of yatris who travel.

Because of that, we are clear about who we take. The yatra is for devotees who are spiritually inclined, who are going to Kailash for Mahadev and not for a holiday. Every registration is screened, and we speak to each yatri before confirming a place, so that like-minded people walk together and the group carries one intention.

Our projects

Seva does not stop at the temple gate. When a need appears where Shiva's devotees are, the foundation turns toward it. These are the projects beyond the kitchens and the yatras.

Nepal flood relief

Ongoing

Nepal is the home of Pashupatinath and the gateway to Kailash. When the floods struck, the seva turned toward the families who had lost everything.

We did not arrive with lorries and leave. Our ground staff first surveyed the affected areas to understand exactly what was needed, household by household. From that survey we selected the families we could genuinely support, and gave each family bespoke help: what that particular family needed, not a standard parcel.

This work is still going on. Our team is still on the ground, still visiting families, still learning what has changed for them since the water went down, and still responding.

Donations for this project go directly to the families our ground team is supporting. Please mark your donation "Nepal relief". Updates from the ground are shared in the newsletter and on the videos page.
